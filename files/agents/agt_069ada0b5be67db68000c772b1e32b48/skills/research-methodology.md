# Research Methodology — Market Intelligence

## What to Actually Search For

### Competition Saturation Check
- Search: `"[keyword] tool" OR "[keyword] software" site:producthunt.com` — count how many launched in last 12 months
- Search: `[keyword] review site:reddit.com` — read what users complain about in existing tools
- Check SimilarWeb estimates for top 3 competitors (search `similarweb [competitor.com]`)
- Check G2, Capterra, Trustpilot for review volume — <100 reviews = early market; >10K = saturated

### Demand Signals (Real, Not Proxy)
- Google Trends for 5-year trajectory — is it growing, flat, or dying?
- Reddit threads asking "how do I [problem]" with >50 upvotes = real demand
- Facebook Group size for the target niche
- "People also ask" on Google for the query — lots of questions = unsolved problem space
- Etsy/Gumroad/AppSumo for digital products — sort by bestsellers and check review counts

### Pricing Intelligence
- Always find 3 real price points before claiming a market exists
- Check AppSumo lifetime deals (signals indie market, not enterprise)
- Check Gumroad bestsellers for digital products — actual $ amounts shown
- For services: check Upwork rates for the skill, Fiverr starting prices, and what agencies charge

### Distribution Reality Check
- Is there a subreddit where the target customer hangs out? What are the rules — can you post?
- Are there Facebook groups with >10K members for this niche?
- Are there newsletters in this space? (search `[niche] newsletter site:substack.com`)
- Is there a ProductHunt category for this? Recent launches getting traction?
- Cold outreach viable? Can you find 50 potential customers on LinkedIn in 1 hour?

### What to Ignore in Research
- Market size reports ($X billion by 2030) — irrelevant when you need $5K/month in 90 days
- Case studies from funded companies — different distribution budget, different rules
- "Top 10 best [tool]" roundups — these are SEO content, not market intelligence
- Twitter/X threads about "I made $10K in a week" — survivorship bias, no sample size

## Output Format for Research Findings
When reporting research:
1. **What I searched** — be specific about queries used
2. **What the data shows** — numbers, not adjectives
3. **Gaps found** — specific unmet needs in user language (quotes from forums/reviews)
4. **Who the actual competitors are** — name, URL, pricing, size signal
5. **Verdict** — one sentence
